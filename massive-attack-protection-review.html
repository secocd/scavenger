Massive Attack - Protection

full review

Artist: Massive Attack

Album: Protection (1994) (49min)

I really like Massive Attack. My parents introduced me to them a couple of years ago. I think my father first made me listen to Teardrop from their album Mezzanine (1998). I wouldn't say I liked it much at first, but after listening to it at different points over the years, I've come to appreciate it more. The songs I listened to the most were Unfinished Sympathy from Blue Lines (1991) and Paradise Circus from Heligoland (2010). Later, I started listening to P Is for Piano from Danny the Dog (2004). After this first introduction, I felt like I was ready to listen to Mezzanine again, and I really liked it this time. I especially enjoyed Angel and Dissolved Girl, which are now my favourite Massive Attack songs. Regarding the album I'm reviewing, Protection (1994), I think I'd only listened to Karmacoma before, but it didn't click at the time. Now that I've listened to the whole album, I can say it surprised me and I like it a lot.

I listened to it about five days ago while studying, and I'm listening to it again now while writing this review. I would give the album a 9/10.

All the songs are good, and the album flows nicely. My favourites are Protection, featuring Tracey Thorn from Everything But The Girl, Karmacoma, Three, Weather Storm, Spying Glass, which has a reggae vibe, and Eurochild. But the other songs are also good. I found it interesting that they included a live cover of Light My Fire by The Doors (1967), which works nicely as the closing track. All in all, I'd say the songs connect perfectly and work really well together, creating a very interesting sound that never gets annoying or repetitive. I also appreciated the different effects they used throughout the album, like the breathing effect in Heat Miser and the reverberating vocals in Three.

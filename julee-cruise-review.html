Julee Cruise - The Voice of Love

full review

Artist: Julee Cruise

Album: The Voice of Love (1993) (51min)

I found this CD in a box full of other CDs, all priced at €2, at a flea market in Berlin. I also own her first album, Floating Into The Night (1989), which I have listened to a lot and it's how I got to discover Twin Peaks. I also really like three songs from her third and last album, The Art of Being a Girl (2010), which are The Orbiting Beatnik, Everybody Knows, and Slow Hot Wind. That album is much more electronic and leans into a downtempo, trip-hop style, completely different from her early stuff. Regarding this album, I never actually got around listening to it but recognised the cover and decided to buy it because I was sure I would like it.

I listened to the album several times with the portable CD player that I recently bought, mostly in my room and at night, which is the best way to listen to her music. It is a very atmospheric album, with a dreamy and slightly unsettling feeling throughout. I would give the album an 8/10.

David Lynch wrote the lyrics, which explains why the whole thing feels so strangely poetic, haunting, and well-structured around its dark, night-time theme. The album does a great job mixing different styles, especially the slightly upbeat, rhythmic grooves on the first tracks like This Is Our Night and one of my favourites, Movin’ in on You, which has a very repetitive and hypnotic rhythm that stays in your head. There is also a nice run of softer, nostalgic songs and lullabies like Friends for Life, Until the End of the World, and In My Other World that keep the dreamy mood going perfectly, alongside the more playful, mysterious swing of Kool Kat Walk and the melancholic, slightly epic feel of The Space for Love. However, my absolute favourite is easily She Would Die for Love. It is just pure, beautiful sadness, and the way the trumpet comes in during the second half completely changes the track, turning the vibe from a ghostly lullaby into a dark, smoky jazz tragedy that gives me chills every single time. On the other side, the sad track Questions in a World of Blue feels a bit simpler and more boring, while the creepy Up in Flames and the title track are way too weak and slow, dragging the energy down. Despite this album not having the same success as her first one, I highly recommend giving it a listen.
